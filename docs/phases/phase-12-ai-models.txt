1. OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Better AI models within the free stack, as the owner asked: refresh the model lists, add
Gemini transcription (chosen by the owner), and survive the free tiers' habits (retired
models, overloaded models). Offline transcription is Phase 13.


2. WHAT CHANGED (AS A USER SEES IT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Google Gemini Transcribe (Settings > AI providers > Transcription)
- Transcripts that say who spoke: "Speaker A", "Speaker B"â€¦ on every line, which no
  Whisper service provides. Rename speakers in the transcript as before.
- Word timings come from the audio, so clicking a line plays exactly that moment.
- The same free key as Gemini for summaries; audio permission is asked separately, with
  a clear warning that Google may use free-tier audio to improve its products.
- Long recordings are sent in 25-minute pieces, so speaker labels stay the same person
  for longer (they restart with each piece; Gemini cannot carry them across requests).

Model choice
- The Model list shows EchoMind's tested models first, then every other suitable model
  the service offers today, read with your key. Offline or without a key, the tested
  list is shown.

Busy models
- When a free model answers "high demand", the request goes to the next Gemini model in
  EchoMind's list instead of failing; Settings are not changed.


3. KEY DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  Gemini transcription was previously rejected (FREE_STACK.md) because prompted
    timestamps are written by the model, not measured. Gemini 3.5 Transcribe is
    different: a dedicated model whose word times are annotations from the audio. It was
    adopted only after a live comparison with Whisper on the same audio (section 5).
D2  The request goes to the Interactions endpoint with the audio inline as Ogg/Opus
    (verified live; the docs only showed file uploads). No Files API upload, so nothing
    is left stored at Google beyond the request itself.
D3  Lines are built from the words: break at a change of speaker, at a sentence end after
    a pause, and at sentence ends past 20 s (40 s at most), which gives short, citable
    lines; Whisper's are paragraph-length.
D4  Pieces are 25 minutes for Gemini (it allows 30 with speakers and timings) and stay 10
    for everyone else, set per provider (maxChunkMs).
D5  Gemini text default stays gemini-3.5-flash-lite: checked live it answered in about a
    second while 3.8 Flash and 3.1 Flash-Lite were "high demand". 3.6 Flash and
    flash-lite-latest were added as fallbacks.
D6  "Busy" is a flag on the error, not a new kind: the queue's handling (wait and retry)
    is unchanged when every model is busy.
D7  Model lists are filtered by purpose, so image, music, robotics, live-audio and other
    specialised models are never offered for summaries; the retired-model fallback uses
    the same filter.


4. BUGS FOUND AND FIXED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F1  Gemini sends its errors as a JSON array ([{"error": â€¦}]). EchoMind read only plain
    objects, so Gemini's own messages ("model no longer available", "high demand") were
    lost and generic text was shown instead.
F2  The retired-model fallback could choose any non-speech model, including image or
    music models a service lists beside its text models.


5. TESTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unit (278 passing): word-to-line building, speaker names, silence, text without
timings, clamping, unfinished replies; the exact request (inline audio, speakers, word
times, key only in its header); array-wrapped errors and "busy" detection; busy fallback
to other models without changing Settings; 25-minute pieces for Gemini.

Live, two-voice meeting (tests/live/gemini-transcribe.spec.ts), 2026-11-23:
                     Gemini 3.5 Transcribe     Groq Whisper
  Facts heard        12 of 12                  12 of 12
  Speakers           2, 100% correct           none
  Lines              21 (sentence by sentence) 9 (paragraphs)
  Time               6.1 s                     3.0 s
  Every turn started within 0.1 s of Whisper's time (6.4, 14.8, 20.7, 39.1, 45.0, 63.0 s).

Live, 60-minute meeting of 10 sessions in 25-minute pieces (LIVE_GEMINI_LONG_MINUTES=60),
2026-11-23: transcribed in 5.3 minutes including two short rate-limit waits, which
EchoMind handled by itself. All 10 session starts found, 9 within 0.2 s and one 3.3 s
early; all 10 facts heard; two speakers.
Speaker labels are the honest weak point: the announcer (always the same voice) was
"Speaker A" for sessions 1 to 4 and "Speaker B" from session 5, a swap inside the first
25-minute piece, and labels also restart between pieces. Labels are reliable for telling
turns apart within a conversation, not for naming one person across a long recording;
the docs say so. Renaming speakers in the transcript still works as before.


6. REVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Designer: speaker labels are the most visible quality gain in this round; the privacy
trade-off is stated where the provider is chosen and again before the first upload.
Developer: one new provider behind the existing interface and pipeline (chunking, queue,
retries, resume); no new dependencies.
Open: the Groq 3-hour stage 2 rerun (Phase 9) still waits for Groq's daily token window,
which had not reset when this phase ended; it moves to the end of Phase 13.
Open: speaker labels restart with each 25-minute piece. Matching voices across pieces
needs voice fingerprints (a candidate: sherpa-onnx, Apache-2.0, offline).
