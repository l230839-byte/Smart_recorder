1. OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let people talk to their recordings, one or many at a time, and get answers that come from
what was actually said, with the moments to prove it. Let them find anything in their library
in one search. Keep improving earlier work along the way.


2. WHAT THIS PHASE INCLUDES (THE FEATURES, AS A USER SEES THEM)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Ask AI (chat)
- Ask about one recording, several chosen ones, a collection (with its sub-collections), or
  the whole library. Start from a recording's page, a Library selection, a collection's
  menu, search results, Home or AI Studio.
- Follow-up questions understand the conversation ("explain it more simply").
- Every statement about a recording cites the moment it came from. Select a time to hear it,
  in whichever recording it is. Sources lists each quoted line with its recording.
- When the recordings do not cover something, the answer says so, with a "Not in your
  recordings" label. General knowledge appears only in a separate, clearly marked box.
- Each answer says what was read: "Read the full transcript", "Read 9 passages from 2 of 14
  recordings", or "Searched 14 recordings; nothing matched".
- Warnings when the AI cited a line it was not given (removed), or when an answer could not
  be linked to any moment.
- Chats are saved, named after their first question, and can be renamed or deleted. Stop
  a running answer; retry a failed one. Starter questions on an empty chat.
- Example questions from the prompt that work: what the teacher explained about a topic,
  "summarize the first 20 minutes", homework, deadlines, decisions, what a speaker said,
  comparing two meetings, finding recordings that discussed a subject.

Search everything (Ctrl+K, or Search at the top of the sidebar)
- One search across recording names, descriptions, tags and notes, transcripts, chapters,
  speaker names, summaries and documents. Urdu and English both work.
- Results grouped by recording, with highlighted snippets and times. A transcript or chapter
  match opens the transcript at that moment; summary and document matches open them.
- Filters: kind of content, collection, and time (past week, month, year).
- "Ask AI about these results" turns a search into a chat about the recordings found.
- Coming Back from a result keeps the search.


3. KEY DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  Read whole transcripts whenever they fit. This grounds answers best and makes "not in
    the recording" trustworthy. The budget follows the provider, capped at 48,000 characters
    to keep answers fast and inside free limits.
D2  Otherwise, retrieve with SQLite FTS5 and BM25 over overlapping passages of about 45 to
    60 seconds, as the prompt allows when embeddings are not yet practical. A short JSON
    "search plan" from the model resolves follow-ups, adds likely words and other-language
    terms, and spots time ranges and whole-recording questions. Several searches are merged
    with reciprocal rank fusion, and the best passage of each recording comes first.
D3  No embeddings in this phase. A free cloud embedding API would mean sending every
    transcript to a third party just to index it (Gemini's free tier may use content to
    improve its products). A local multilingual model is the better option, as an optional
    add-on like FFmpeg. Recorded as the main follow-up (L3).
D4  Lines are numbered S1, S2… across all recordings in the request, so the model cites one
    short id and EchoMind knows exactly which recording and moment it means. Citations are
    checked against the exact lines sent.
D5  Answers come back as JSON that separates the recordings' answer, whether it was found,
    and general knowledge. One retry for unreadable JSON; plain text is accepted only if it
    is not broken JSON.
D6  Chat runs straight away, not in the job queue, so a question never waits behind a
    long transcription. It passes the same offline, key and consent checks. Short rate limits
    (20 seconds or less) are waited out once; longer ones say when to try again.
D7  Privacy: a chat records every recording whose text was sent, cited or not. Deleting a
    recording deletes all of those chats (§21).
D8  Search groups by recording and ranks within each recording in SQL, so a word used often
    in a few recordings cannot crowd out the rest.


4. FIXES AND IMPROVEMENTS MADE WHILE BUILDING AND REVIEWING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
F1  [Bug, search quality] Global search took the best 1,500 transcript lines overall, so a
    common phrase filled them from about 30 recordings and silently dropped the others. Found
    by the 300-hour test. Now ranked per recording with window functions, with true match
    counts. Unit test added.
F2  [Bug, upgrade] Right after updating, a library-wide question could miss older
    transcripts whose passages were still being built. Chat now indexes anything it needs
    first, and the startup pass works in small batches so the app stays responsive.
F3  [Bug, crash] Newer Chromium returns a promise from scrollIntoView. An effect returned it
    and React treated it as a cleanup, which crashed the chat screen. Fixed, and the whole app
    was checked for the same pattern.
F4  [Privacy] Chats first recorded only the recordings they cited. Any recording whose text
    was sent now counts, so deleting it deletes the chat. Found by a flaky test, now stable
    over 15 runs.
F5  [Reliability] If the database closed while an answer was finishing (during quit),
    saving the failure threw an unhandled error. Guarded; the answer is marked interrupted at
    next launch.
F6  [UX] Back from a search result lost the query. It is now kept.
F7  [Earlier phase, summaries] Citations written as ranges, like [S12-S15], stayed in
    summaries as raw text. They now link both ends. Tested.
F8  [Earlier phases] Links can now point at another recording's moment; copying text handles
    them. Home has an Ask action. The Library selection, collections and AI Studio gained
    Ask AI.
F9  [Tooling] The demo model now answers chat with the lines that share the most words with
    the question, and says when none do, so the whole flow works with no key.
F10 [Tests] A test helper waited a fixed number of ticks and was slow (9 s) and
    timing-dependent; it now waits for the real condition (under 1 s).

7. UNRESOLVED / KNOWN LIMITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
L1  NOT YET RUN ON LIVE GROQ OR GEMINI. Needs the owner's key in desktop/.env.live, then
    `npm run test:live`. I will read the report and judge transcription, summaries,
    documents and chat against the known facts.
L2  Urdu quality cannot be judged with generated speech: Windows here has no Urdu voice. An
    Urdu recording from the owner would close this.
L3  Matching is by words, not meaning. A question that shares no words with the transcript
    can miss the right passage when the library is too large to read whole. The search plan
    reduces this with likely words and translations. Local semantic search (a small
    multilingual model, downloaded on request) is the planned upgrade.
L4  Very large scopes are searched, not read whole, so a question about "everything" in a big
    library sees an even sample or the best passages, and the answer says what it read.
L5  Answers are not streamed word by word; they appear when complete (usually a few seconds).
L6  Everything else from earlier reports still applies.
