ECHOMIND DESKTOP: PHASE 11, THEMES
================================================================================
Status:      COMPLETE.
Prompt refs: §18 design (themes, accessibility); §16 settings
Date:        2026-11-23


1. OBJECTIVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Polish light and dark, and add accent colours and high contrast, as the owner asked
("all 3 options"), with every combination readable.


2. WHAT CHANGED (AS A USER SEES IT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Settings > Appearance now has three choices:
- Theme: System, Light or Dark (as before).
- Accent colour: indigo (default), teal, rose, amber, green or slate, as round swatches
  (arrow keys move between them). Buttons, links, the selected page and the EchoMind
  mark take the accent.
- Contrast: System, Standard or High. High uses black on white (or white on black with
  yellow actions, as Windows does), strong outlines and no shadows. System switches to
  high contrast whenever Windows asks for more contrast.

Polish:
- Light: cool "ink on paper" greys instead of warm cream. Warm cream is a very common
  default and sat oddly beside the cool indigo brand.
- Dark: buttons are a saturated colour with white text; before, a pale lavender with
  dark text made the main action look disabled. Page, surface and raised surfaces
  (menus, dialogs) are three distinct levels, and shadows keep a faint edge.
- Focus rings use their own colour, light enough to see on dark pages.
- Changing theme, accent or contrast cross-fades briefly (not with reduced motion).


3. KEY DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D1  Primary has two roles. --primary is the solid fill and always carries --on-primary
    text; --primary-text is the accent as text on the page. In dark they differ (a
    saturated fill for white text; a light tint for text on a dark page). Every text use
    of the old token moved to --primary-text in one mechanical change.
D2  Accents and contrast are attributes on <html> (data-accent, data-contrast) read by
    tokens.css; light or dark stays with Electron's theme, which already drives
    prefers-color-scheme and the title bar. High contrast comes last, so it always wins.
D3  Soft tints disappear in high contrast, so tinted shapes (icon tiles, selected items,
    tonal buttons, notices) get an outline there instead.
D4  Every pair was checked before building (palette sheet shown to the owner: button
    text, links on page and card, tinted chips, muted text, and controls against the
    page). The one failure, slate's dark button against the page (2.7:1), was fixed
    (3.4:1) before anything was applied.
D5  Settings with fixed choices (theme, accent, contrast, audio quality) now refuse any
    other value instead of storing it; before, any string was accepted.


4. TESTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Accessibility (axe, WCAG 2.1 A/AA): every main screen in light and dark, plus Home,
  a recording, AI Studio and Settings in each of the five other accents in light and
  dark, and in high contrast light and dark: 0 problems.
- Unit: settings refuse unlisted values (271 passing). Full end-to-end suite passing.
- Screens reviewed by eye: light, dark, dark teal, light rose, high contrast light and
  dark on Home, a recording, Summaries, AI Studio and Settings.


5. REVIEW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Designer: dark mode's main actions now read as main actions; high contrast follows the
conventions of Windows' own contrast themes. The accent changes the app's character
without touching the fixed meanings (teal for AI, ember for bookmarks, red for record).
Developer: one token file, no colours in components except the swatches (which must show
their own colour); canvases redraw on any appearance change.
